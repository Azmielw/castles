# Pace and motion: the standard (new in 1.7.0, 2026-11-12)

Where these numbers come from: five reference videos the owner of Khaam chose (four narrated religious and reflective videos and one shop ad), measured frame by frame and word by word against Khaam's own first six samples. The samples stood still (0.3–3.3% of the screen moving, against 5.5–12.3% in the references), paused for a third of their speech (31–34% silence, against 0–9%), and talked slower (1.72–1.78 words a second, against 1.9–2.3). The six were rebuilt to the numbers below, and the owner's verdict was «ممتاز… عجبني البدايات، لفت الانتباه، سرعة الكلام، التوجيه المباشر». So these numbers are the default for every video the skill makes. A video that misses one says why in its `TASTE.md`.

## What the viewer gets in the first second
- **The first frame already holds them.** Narrated: the first word by 0.1 s, spoken to the viewer («يومك…», «كل زبون…», «وش الشي اللي…»). With no voice: something visibly moves by 0.3 s. The words on screen are readable by 0.2 s, and the subject is in frame 0 (no fade from black, no logo first). This is also the cover frame: make it the question or the claim.
- **Speak to the viewer, directly.** «إنت»، «يومك»، «جاوب في بالك»: a line addressed to the person watching, not a narrator describing someone else. A story told in the first person («كل زبون يجي…») is direct too.

## The numbers (P1–P13)
| | What | The number |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | the first frame | narrated: the first word by 0.1 s; no voice: the first visible motion by 0.3 s; words readable by 0.2 s; the subject in frame 0 |
| **P2** | speech rate | 1.9–2.4 words a second over the spoken span, reached by cutting silence, **never by time-stretch** (the owner liked 2.35 and 2.38) |
| **P3** | pauses | true silence inside the speech at most 10% of the spoken span; one held beat of 0.5–0.8 s per 30 s; every other gap 0.35 s or less; a short floor of real silence kept at each sentence end (about 0.15 s) so the last consonant stays clear. In a new script «…» at most once per 15 s (a designed voice stops 0.6–0.8 s on each), «،» for a breath |
| **P4** | picture changes | a change of at least 12% of the screen (a new place, a cut, a reframing move, a big object arriving) on average every 4.5 s or less, never more than 8 s apart |
| **P5** | big changes | at least 30% of the screen changes at least every 8 s |
| **P6** | screen in motion | at least 4% of the screen moving on average; aim for 5–8% |
| **P7** | dead seconds | a second where under 1% of the screen moves is dead. Narrated: at most 25% of the seconds, no run over 4 s. No voice: at most 10%, no run over 2 s. The end holds 2–2.5 s at most and keeps breathing (light, a sway, a slow push) |
| **P8** | the camera | a visible move in every scene (a push or pull of 1%/s or more, or a pan, rise or drop of 30 px/s or more at 1080 wide), on 30–45% of the running time (visible moves only: a slow breathing push under every shot does not count toward it, so a style that never stands still still lands here); a push meant to read as motion is 2.5–4%/s; background layers in a travel shot move 40–250 px/s in parallax; at least one move per video carries the meaning on a word (walls part on «أوسع», a dive on «يونس», a rise on the dua). No shake. On a pale page (a notebook, a board) P6 and P7 also need the world to breathe between the visible moves: a slow drift of the page, a sheet's sway, ink being written; that counts as motion, not toward this share (a blind lesson of 11-12 failed P6 and P7 with the camera at 44% until it did) |
| **P9** | places | a new place or a new framing every sentence or two (`Khaam.places` in the scene kit) |
| **P10** | words on screen | either the whole sentence appears and each word lights as it is spoken (`word-lit.html`), or one big word at a time follows the voice (a chain story, an ad). A new sentence at least every 4 s. No «،» «.» «:» «!» and no harakat on your own lines; «…» and a real «؟» stay |
| **P11** | figures | the figure that carries the story is about 430 px tall (of 1920) and acts in every scene (walks, reaches, works, sits down, turns), never only bobbing. Seated = upright; bending = standing, 30° or less at the waist, head up; both knees down with the chest forward only in a prayer scene. Head: a plain ghutra or a kufi (the igal only when asked). Faceless (`reference/figure-kit.md`) |
| **P12** | text on top | moving backgrounds must not lower contrast: a soft plate under any line over motion (`S.plate`, word-lit's `plate`), contrast checked on the brightest frame. And the text never covers the picture: the words keep their own band, and at every camera stop each card, number and drawing rests whole below it (`explainer-reel-system.md` §2) |
| **P13** | the check | `check_pace.py` on the draft render (below), next to `check_mix.py`; write the numbers in `TASTE.md` |

A still verified card (an ayah, a hadith, a dua) stays whole and still while it is read: the world behind it keeps moving (the camera, light, small life), and the check does not count the card's span against P4 and P5.

## Plan with it (before any frame is built)
1. **The opening line and frame first:** the question or the claim, said to the viewer, readable at frame 0, spoken from frame 0 (P1).
2. **The script for the pace:** about 2.1 words a second of the length you want (a 30 s video ≈ 60–65 words); «…» once at most per 15 s; one held beat placed on purpose (before the turn or the answer).
3. **A shot list by sentence:** for each sentence or two, a new place or framing (P9), the camera move in it (P8: what moves, how fast), what the figure does there (P11), and which word carries the meaning move. Mark where the picture changes by 12% or more: every 4.5 s on average (P4), a big one every 8 s (P5). **A lesson on one page** (a notebook, a board, a sheet) needs them too: a section on tinted paper sliding in, a big drawing (a third of the frame or more) arriving, a push-in on the number, or a cut to a fresh line. A scroll down the same pale page, or pale cards landing on pale paper, is no change to the viewer (a blind test on 11-12: one page for 26 s, one change).
4. **The voice before the picture** in a narrated video: generate the whole take, tighten it (`voice_edit.py tighten`: P2–P3), then time every beat from its words (`cues.py`), never the other way round (`reference/voiceover.md`).
5. **The sound plan last:** real recordings on the moments that matter, in the pauses (`reference/film-sound.md`). More motion never means more sounds.

## Review with it (on the draft render)
```
python "<skill>/scripts/check_pace.py" "renders/previews/<name> - Preview v1.mp4" --plan plan/sound.json
python "<skill>/scripts/check_pace.py" "renders/previews/<name> - Preview v1.mp4"         # a video with no voice and no plan
python "<skill>/scripts/check_pace.py" "<video>.mp4" --voice-file assets/vo/vo.wav --at 0.02  # narrated, no sound plan yet
```
It prints every number against its limit: **FAIL** for P1, P2, P3 (true silence), P6 and P7 (fix them before the user sees the video); **WARN** for a near miss on P4 and P5, the gaps, the end hold and the band measure (fix them, or write the reason in `TASTE.md`); **FAIL** for a gross miss on P4 or P5 (since 11-12): a mean gap between 12% changes over 9 s or any gap over 16 s (twice the limits); no 30% change at all over a judged stretch longer than 10 s, or a gap over 16 s. The FAIL line names the fix. **A near-miss WARN may stand only with its reason in `TASTE.md`,** said plainly: a verified card held still while it is read (its span goes to the check as `"scripture"` in the plan, or `--quote`), a dark scene whose real changes stay under 25 grey levels (say which seconds), or one gap a fraction of a second over. **A pale page is never a reason:** cards a few grey levels off pale paper are no change to the eye either; on a pale page the check says so and counts the faint changes it leaves out. **info** for the camera (the probe misses parallax, slow pushes and flat art: read the composition's own camera timeline and write its share) and for P9–P12 (look at the contact sheet). With `--plan` it reads the voice, its start and the quoted-text spans from the sound plan, and the word times from the take's `.words.json`. It decodes the video small (10 fps, 216×384), so it runs in seconds and needs no render lock.

How it measures: screen in motion = the share of pixels that change by more than 8 grey levels in 0.1 s; a picture change = at least 12% (or 30%) of the screen changing by more than 25 levels within 0.5 s, or changing colour (Cb/Cr) by more than 12 (since 11-12: a light blue or peach section slides in over cream paper only 10–13 grey levels apart, and the eye sees it; the colour adds such a change, never merges two into one); a dead second = under 1% moving on average. True silence = the voice's 250–3800 Hz band and its full band both 30 dB under its median for 0.25 s or more. The band measure (15 dB under, the direction sheet's first probe) also counts a word's last consonant and a breath, so it reads higher (14–17% on the rebuilt samples where the true silence was 2–6%): it is shown beside it and warns over 18%.

Measured on the six rebuilt samples (2026-11-12, all PASS): 2.05–2.38 words a second; true silence 0–5%; 4.1–8.3% of the screen in motion; 3–18% dead seconds, no run over 3 s. Their 11-11 versions all FAIL: 0.33–3.3% in motion, up to 41 of 42 seconds dead. Picture changes on the six (the gross-miss rule above was set from them): P4 mean gap 2.5–5.6 s, longest 4.4–7.9 s; P5 longest gap 5.5–8.3 s; three of them carry a near-miss WARN with its reason (01: one night shot under the dua card; 03: one gap 0.2 s over; 04: dark night and soil scenes under 25 grey levels). A blind buyer-test video of 11-12 read 2.5 s and 5.2 s; a blind lesson on one pale notebook page read one change in 26 s and none of 30%: it now FAILS.

A pass means the numbers hold, not that the video holds attention: say so, and let the user judge the whole video.
